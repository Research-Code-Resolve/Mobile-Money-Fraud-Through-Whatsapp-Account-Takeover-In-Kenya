# PROBLEM 2: MOBILE MONEY FRAUD THROUGH WHATSAPP ACCOUNT TAKEOVER IN KENYA
## The Problem:
Mobile money users in Kenya face a critical gap when their WhatsApp accounts are taken over and used to ask for money from trusted contacts. Victims often lose control of their line due to SIM swap or account compromise and cannot quickly alert others or stop the fraud. During this short window, contacts send money through services like M-Pesa, assuming the request is genuine. The lack of a fast, accessible response tool creates delays, leading to preventable financial loss and prolonged recovery.
## Who Is Affected:
All mobile money users are at risk, especially those in urban, peri-urban, and rural areas including informal settlements. This includes informal workers, the elderly, and individuals who rely on M-Pesa for daily transactions. Their social networks are tightly knit, which makes them more vulnerable to trust-based scams. The majority use basic or mid-range phones, and the elderly often depend on M-Pesa agents or neighbors for support, which limits their access to complex digital security tools.
## What Has Been Tried:
Kenya already has fraud prevention tools, but they mostly focus on the period before or after the fraud occurs, not during it. Safaricom provides customer care lines and reversal processes, but these take time and sometimes require a physical shop visit or access to the affected line. The Communications Authority, banks, and civil society organizations lead awareness campaigns, but these rely on user vigilance, which fails under urgency and social trust. Security features like PINs and two-factor authentication do not help once an account is already compromised. None of these solutions provide an immediate, low-tech way for victims to alert their networks to stop fraud in real time.
## Where Technology Could Help:
Close the response gap during active fraud. A USSD-based system can provide immediate access to an emergency trigger that works across all phones, including basic devices, without internet. SMS infrastructure can enable instant alerts to trusted contacts, breaking the trust cycle that fraudsters rely on. Simple automation can guide victims through clear recovery steps linked to providers like Safaricom, reducing confusion and delay. Data collected from incidents can be used to map common fraud patterns and improve early detection over time.
